# Explain the problem

AI can now describe heart and brain studies in fluent language, but the people who have to rely on it can't check
where any of it came from. Which measurement drove the statement, which threshold was applied, which guideline says
so? A clinician shown a confident AI summary can't tell a correct grade from an invented number. A lab researcher
running dozens or hundreds of records through a model has no audit trail, so the results are hard to reproduce, compare
or publish. Tested on our own cases, an LLM reading raw records got **0 % of guideline grades right and invented
numbers**. Capability isn't the bottleneck for AI in clinical work and research; **trust and traceability** are. Health
data is also messy: values come with missing units or incomplete fields, and free-text notes can carry hidden
instructions to the AI. A useful tool has to say what it doesn't know rather than guess. COR Studio is **not a medical
device and gives no diagnosis**. It is decision support for physicians, an assistant to the doctor, and a transparent
analysis tool for researchers, built on synthetic and open data.
